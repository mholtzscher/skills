# Explore alternatives

Explore competing approaches when a novel UI interaction or architectural decision has no clear precedent in the codebase. This also applies to consequential product choices whose experience depends on feel rather than logic alone.

Define the decision criterion before building candidates. Compare a simple option with credible alternatives, including ownership and verification costs. Two or three competing prototypes viewed side by side can be useful when they expose meaningfully different experiences.

Use parallel proposals or isolated prototypes when they can produce different evidence. An ordinary function boundary does not require a panel.

Stop when the deciding uncertainty is resolved. State which observation favored the chosen design and what remains unknown. Do not manufacture alternatives for a settled mechanical choice or continue prototypes after the evidence is sufficient.
