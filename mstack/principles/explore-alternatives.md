# Explore alternatives

Explore competing approaches when a novel UI interaction or architectural decision has no clear precedent in the codebase. This also applies to consequential product choices whose experience depends on feel rather than logic alone. A plausible design on paper may not settle which interaction is understandable or satisfying in use.

Define the decision criterion before building candidates. Compare a simple option with credible alternatives, including ownership and verification costs. Two or three competing prototypes viewed side by side can be useful when they expose meaningfully different experiences. That is a comparison shape, not a candidate quota for every decision.

Use parallel proposals or isolated prototypes when they can produce different evidence. Approve executable prototype work before building it. Use available host-configured delegation only when it helps. An ordinary function boundary does not require a panel.

Stop when the deciding uncertainty is resolved. State which observation favored the chosen design and what remains unknown. Do not manufacture alternatives for a settled mechanical choice or continue prototypes after the evidence is sufficient.
